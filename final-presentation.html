GovTech Singapore · Final Project

ServiceSG policy finder (Scenario A)

The assistant finds the three most relevant policies for a resident enquiry and drafts a first reply, so officers spend less time searching and more time checking.

GovTech Singapore onsite · November 2026

What I built

Twelve modules, one use case

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

A product manager and analyst for ServiceSG. Owns service outcomes, not just metrics, and weighs quality, speed, resident experience and risk.

Task

Find where ServiceSG falls short on reply time, quality and repeat contacts. Separate facts from guesses, rank fixes by impact and effort, and say what to test before committing.

Module 2
Prototype
What it demonstrates

That simple keyword matching puts the right policy in the top three for clear enquiries, using 50 synthetic policies. The risky part is scale: it must still work at 500+ policies, or officers will stop trusting it.

Debrief

Clear queries like "CPF housing withdrawal" matched well, and held up to 250 policies. Vague queries, synonyms and typos did not. Next I will test at 500+ policies and add meaning-based search before building a real backend.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

ServiceSG officers answer enquiries across CPF, HDB, healthcare, education and employment. They spend more time finding the right policy than writing the reply, which slows replies and makes answers inconsistent.

Permissions

Read: the policy index, risk tags and the enquiry text. Draft: the top three policies with scores and a reply draft. Write: audit log entries only. Send: never. The officer picks the policy and sends the reply. No access to resident personal data.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

When the officer picks a policy, the agent checks its risk tag. Standard policies go straight to a draft. High-risk policies get a draft but the send button stays locked until a supervisor reviews it.

Rules of engagement

The agent never picks the policy and never sends. High-risk cases, such as ComCare appeals, HDB repossession or MediShield denials, need a verified supervisor ID. Every step and any bypass attempt is logged.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Everything the officer sees for one enquiry: the top three policies, any warning, the draft, the send button and the log. The one job: put the right policy in front of the officer with a safe draft.

Pass bar

The right policy is in the top three on at least 80% of 14 cases. Zero fails on the safety questions. An average of 4 out of 5 on "easy to act on". Below 60% on the right policy, I stop.

Module 12

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Officers find the right policy in seconds and start from a safe draft, while they stay in charge of every decision and every send.

Baseline

3 minutes per ticket to find the right policy; 69.1% of requests resolved first time. In the eval, the right policy was in the top three on 73% of cases after fixes. All numbers are synthetic.

Target and review date

1 minute per ticket, the right policy in the top three on 80% of cases, and zero safety fails. Review on 21 December 2026.

Usage metric

How often officers pick one of the three recommended policies. Target: half of pilot officers use it on at least 1 in 5 tickets within two weeks.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Too many low-confidence warnings. If the warning shows on more than half of tickets, officers will learn to ignore it, so I’ll retune it with the Engineering Lead.

Stop line

If the right policy is in the top three on fewer than 60% of cases, or one wrong reply reaches a resident, I pause the assistant and officers go back to manual search.

Reflection

What we learned

What our team of four found hardest, what we’ll keep doing, and the moment our thinking changed.

Friction

The AI was the easy part.

Getting the AI to produce an output was quick. The hard part was everything around it: guardrails, handling failures, edge cases and evals. A step-by-step framework helped, but there was a lot to read and cross-check so the skill file, PRD, rubric and pass bars told the same story, and users could trust the output.

Key learnings

Fundamentals first, in small steps.

Start with the problem and the riskiest assumption, not the technology. Plan the golden path and don’t skip the fundamentals for convenience. Break the work into steps, build small, test early, and measure real outcomes, not just whether the AI works. Keep humans in control, make uncertainty visible, and set a clear point to stop.

Aha moment

Work backwards from what good looks like.

Define the users, deal-breakers and pass bars first, so evals shape what we build, not just test it. Trust matters most in government services. Under the complexity it still comes back to business requirements. The PM role has changed, and it felt like going back to school, but our product judgment still counts. AI is one more tool in the PM toolkit.
