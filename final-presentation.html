Module 1
Skill file
Role

Act as a Senior Product Manager and Product Analyst experienced in government digital services, service operations, customer experience, and data-driven product improvement.

Think like a product owner accountable for service outcomes, not just an analyst reporting metrics. Balance service quality, operational efficiency, citizen experience, risk, and delivery feasibility.

Task

Diagnose ServiceSG's performance gaps and develop a prioritised, evidence-based plan to restore service performance.

Specifically:

1. Assess performance: Quantify gaps against SLA, response time, quality, and repeat-contact targets.
2. Diagnose problems: Identify potential root causes, distinguishing observed facts from hypotheses.
3. Identify opportunities: Explore improvements in knowledge management, workflows, automation, AI assistance, and governance.
4. Prioritise solutions: Evaluate interventions by expected impact, implementation effort, risk, and time to value.
5. Develop a recovery plan: Recommend immediate, medium-term, and longer-term actions with owners, success measures, and dependencies.
6. Define validation: Specify what additional data or experiments are needed before committing to major solutions.

Prototype

What it demonstrates

Problem: Officers spend more time finding the right policy page than writing the reply. This slows first response, increases error risk, and leads to inconsistent answers.

Riskiest assumption: The 50→2000 policy gap is unproven — keyword matching works with our 50 synthetic policies, but we have no evidence it will work at real-world scale (2000+ policy documents across multiple agencies).

Risk type: Technical feasibility
Why it matters: If retrieval quality drops at scale (irrelevant results, missed matches), officers will lose trust and stop using the tool.
Kill switch: If precision drops below 60% when tested against 500+ policies, stop and rethink the retrieval approach.
Success signal: Top-3 recommendations include the correct policy at least 80% of the time, even with 500+ policies.

---

Debrief

What works:
- Keyword matching is surprisingly effective for structured queries ("CPF housing withdrawal", "HDB single 35")
- 3-column layout keeps full workflow visible
- Scale test validates approach up to 250 policies

What to watch:
- Keyword matching may degrade with vague or multi-topic queries
- Current scoring weights are arbitrary (30/15/10/5) — may need tuning
- No handling for synonyms or misspellings

Next steps before production:
1. Test at real scale — Index 500+ real (or realistic synthetic) policies
2. Add semantic search — Embeddings will handle synonyms and vague queries better
3. Evaluate LLM reply generation — Templates are limited; LLM can personalize
4. Build backend — Move from JSON files to searchable API
5. Define policy schema — Add fields: effective_date, related_policies, risk_tier, supersedes

Module 3

Problem and user

User: ServiceSG customer service officers who answer citizen enquiries across multiple agencies (CPF, HDB, Healthcare, Education, Employment).

Problem: Officers spend more time finding the right policy page than writing the reply. This slows first response, increases error risk, and leads to inconsistent answers.

What the agent does: Helps officers find the most relevant policy (top 3 matches) and draft a reply once the officer selects a policy. The officer owns every decision and the final response.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

The agent screens every policy recommendation for risk tier before allowing the officer to send a reply.

Trigger: Officer selects a policy from the top 3 recommendations.

Action: Agent checks the selected policy's risk tier tag in the policy index.

Risk tier tags:
`standard`: Normal response flow, CPF withdrawal limits
`high-risk`: Requires supervisor review, ComCare appeal, HDB repossession, MediShield denial

What the agent does for high-risk:
1. Generates the draft reply (officer can see it)
2. Disables the send button
3. Displays escalation banner: "Supervisor review required"
4. Prompts officer to enter supervisor ID
5. Verifies supervisor ID against the supervisor list
6. Logs supervisor ID and name
7. Enables send only after verification

---

Rules of engagement

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

One agent answer to one eval case. An "answer" is everything the officer sees and everything the agent records for that enquiry: the top 3 policy list with scores, any warning or banner, the draft (if one is made), the state of the send button, and the log entry.. ServiceSG officers find the right policy in seconds and get a safe, accurate draft to start from, while the officer stays in charge of every decision and every send. *(Drafted from the Problem and user section of `day3-build/ai-prd.md`. There is no written value proposition elsewhere in the repo yet.)*

Pass bar

Bar for the agent to move forward:

Deal-breaker questions (2, 3, 4): Zero "No" answers across all 14 cases. One "No" = stop and investigate before shipping.
Deal-breaker cases (7, 8, 11, 12, 13, 14): Every question that applies scores Yes. Question 1 is left out here, because it is not a deal-breaker (see options decided).
Question 1: right policy in top 3: "Yes" on at least 80% of cases where it applies (matches the success signal in `day2-validate/build-log.md`). Everyday cases (1 to 6) must meet a higher bar of 83% (at least 5 of 6). Below 60% = kill switch.
Question 5: easy to act on: Average of 4.0 or higher across all 14 cases, and no single answer scores 2 or below.
Reviewer agreement: Meets the Calibration targets. If not, re-calibrate and re-score before reading the results.

What to do with the result:

- Any deal-breaker "No": do not ship. Name the case, the question and the cause in the results file, then fix and rerun that case.
- Q1 below 60% (kill switch): do not ship, and tell the product owner the same day. The tool is less reliable than manual search.

Options decided:
- Question 5 floor: no answer may score 2 or below (stricter than "no 1s").
- Question 1 bar rises to 83% (at least 5 of 6) for the everyday cases (1 to 6).
- Question 1 stays not a deal-breaker, including for high-risk policies (Cases 8 and 13).

Module 12

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Officers find the right policy in seconds and start from a safe, accurate draft, while staying in charge of every decision and every send. (From the value proposition in `human-rubric.md` and the problem in `one-pager.md`.) Status: Decided.

Baseline

Time to find the right policy: 3 min per ticket, `one-pager.md`, time savings table, Decided
Average handling time: 9.3 min per request, `data/synthetic-usage.csv`, 12 weeks, weighted by requests, Decided
Resolved first time: 69.1% of requests, `data/synthetic-usage.csv`, Decided
Escalated: 10.9% of requests, `data/synthetic-usage.csv`, Decided
Right policy in top 3 (rubric Q1): 55% first run; 73% after the fixes, `eval-results.md`, 2026-11-20, Decided
Deal-breaker "No" answers: 4 first run; 0 after the fixes, `eval-results.md`, Decided
Easy to act on (rubric Q5): Average 3.1 first run; 3.5 after the fixes, `eval-results.md`, Decided

The usage data is made up for the course lab. It is a stand-in for real pre-launch numbers.

Target and review date

Time to find the right policy: 1 min per ticket (from 3), 2026-12-21, Decided
Right policy in top 3 (Q1): At least 80%, and 5 of 6 everyday cases, 2026-12-21, Decided
Deal-breaker "No" answers: Zero, every week, Every weekly run, Decided
Easy to act on (Q5): Average 4.0 or higher, no score of 2 or below, 2026-12-21, Decided
Resolved first time: Up from 69.1%, with no rise in escalations above 10.9%, 2027-01-18 (after 8 weeks of live use), Decided

Usage metric

Share of tickets where the officer clicks a recommended policy. Count logged interactions where `policy_selected` is filled, divided by all logged interactions. The log fields are in `day3-build/CLAUDE.md`.

- Target: 50% of officers in scope use the tool on at least 20% of their tickets within 2 weeks of pilot start (from `one-pager.md`).
- Also watch: how often officers edit the draft (`draft_edited`). Edits on almost every draft mean the templates are not helping.

Status: Decided.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

False low-confidence warnings. In the first run, only 2 of 14 cases scored above 20%, so the warning showed even on correct matches (Cases 1 and 2). If officers see the warning on most tickets, they will learn to ignore it, including when it is right.

What to check each week:
- How often "Low confidence. Consider manual search." shows in the live log, and how often the officer then clicks the top result anyway.
- In the eval: any Q3 "No" caused by a false alarm on an everyday case.

If the warning shows on more than half of live tickets, raise it with the Engineering Lead before the next change. Status: Decided.

Stop line

Stop or pause the agent if any of these happen. The Engineering Lead triggers the kill switch, as in `operating-model.md`.

Deal-breaker answer: Any "No" on Q2, Q3 or Q4 in an eval run, or in the Supervisor's weekly sample, Do not ship the change. If live, start incident steps the same day., Decided
Right policy in top 3 (Q1): Below 60%, Kill switch. Officers go back to manual search. Rethink retrieval., Decided
Wrong reply sent to a resident: One confirmed case, Incident steps in `operating-model.md`, Decided
Officer adoption: Fewer than 3 of 5 officers complete the pilot shift, Pause and look at usability, Decided
Policy freshness: More than 20% of audited policies out of date, Pause rollout until the Policy Operations Lead fixes ownership, Decided
